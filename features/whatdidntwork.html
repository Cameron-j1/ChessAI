What Didn't Work

Categorical Move Prediction

Our initial approach using categorical move prediction faced several limitations:

  • Arbitrary Move Encoding: Assigning numbers to moves created an artificial representation that didn't reflect the spatial nature of chess
  • Limited Learning: The model struggled to learn meaningful patterns due to the disconnect between input and output representations
  • Poor Generalization: The categorical approach made it difficult for the model to understand relationships between similar moves
  • Memory Inefficiency: Required maintaining a large mapping dictionary between moves and their categorical values

Deep Q-Learning Network (DQN)

Our DQN implementation faced several challenges:

  • Training Complexity: The model struggled to converge due to the complexity of chess state space
  • Alpha-Beta Integration: Difficulties in effectively combining Alpha-Beta pruning with the DQN architecture
  • Checkmate Search: The dedicated search algorithm for winning combinations proved less effective than traditional methods
  • Experience Replay: Memory management issues with storing and learning from past games
  • Reward Shaping: Challenges in balancing immediate rewards with long-term strategic goals

The training process encountered issues with:

  • Inconsistent performance in self-play games
  • Difficulty in maintaining stable learning rates
  • Limited improvement against baseline engines
  • Computational resource constraints

Continuous Learning

The model faced challenges in continuous improvement through:

  • Limited success in incorporating new high-quality games
  • Performance degradation over time
  • Feature engineering complexity
